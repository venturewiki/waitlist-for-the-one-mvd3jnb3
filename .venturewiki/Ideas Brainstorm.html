The idea in one line

An AI matchmaker that finds exactly the person you described, and you only pay when that person exists. No swiping, no chat, no subscription. You describe your ideal partner (by voice, to an AI agent), wait on a free waitlist, get told "we found 5 people who match", unlock one for about $29, and the app books a date straight from both calendars. To unlock the next match you have to actually show up, checked in at the venue.

Working name from the call: “Waitlist for The One”. Logo: an hourglass whose falling sand turns into a heart (the 30-day match cycle). The concept came from a recurring dream the founder had about "a platform that picked someone exactly within the profile I had chosen."

The story from the call

Two people are on the call: the founder (owner of the idea — "eu que sou a criadora disso", she previously ran an eyewear company) and Beto / Roberto (NYC, product and tech partner). The first ~80 minutes are personal conversation and are left out of this document. The app discussion starts at 1:19 and runs to the end.

  1. The problem. Dating apps are swipe-and-chat loops: people ghost, flake, collect matches for ego, and never meet. Beto's own examples: a woman who postponed twice by WhatsApp; being reported as a "scammer" on Hinge the day after a date.
  2. The spark. A friend (Vanessa) wanted to know who someone is "professionally" before meeting. Beto pointed to Tawkify-style human matchmaking (≈$5k for 5 matches). The founder's answer: do that with AI and make it accessible to everyone ("acessível pro Zé ou Pedro"), not a luxury service.
  3. The rule that changes behavior. You get very few matches, and you can only buy the next one after proving you met the current one. This "solves the biggest problem on both sides: these two people have to actually meet" (Beto, 1:32).
  4. Curiosity is the engine. The app tells you how many people fit your description (5, 3, 15, 22 — never "99+"). Scarcity makes it feel special; the next month's count keeps people coming back.
  5. Debate on price. Beto argued for higher prices (Tawkify, Ohlala). The founder insisted on impulse-level pricing ("two taps and you bought it") to win volume. They settled on low per-match pricing with an optional priority boost.
  6. Debate on payment timing. Charging at match time fails when the other side isn't around. Resolution: buy match credits up front when you enter your requirements; credits are debited when a match is made; the waitlist itself is free.
  7. Debate on proof of meeting. GPS, phone-tap/NFC and screenshots were rejected (privacy fears, AI-faked images, one side lying). They converged on a check-in at the venue on arrival, before the date starts — QR code exchange or a quick photo — confirmed by both.
  8. Next steps agreed. Pick a name and logo (Gemini / ChatGPT), and have Beto's AI assistant "Alex" draft the requirements with the founder.

Where this idea is genuinely different

The meet-to-unlock rule (no next match without a confirmed meeting), the venue check-in, no chat at all (calendar booking only), first-to-accept dispatch across candidates, and an impulse price ($29 vs Sitch's $89.99+ and Tawkify's thousands). Voice onboarding and hidden photos alone are no longer unique — Known launched with both.

Open decisions

Waitlist: free or $0.99?

Founder: $0.99 captures the card for one-tap charges. Beto: most people won't enter a card at all; go free and charge at unlock. Recommendation: free, card at first unlock.

Price points

$29 / $50 for 2 / +$10 third, vs $30–40 base, vs Beto's case for higher prices. Women and men same price? (Ohlala charges only men; left unresolved.)

Timers

12 h to accept, 24 h to book — or 48 h / 5–10 days. Shorter keeps momentum; longer is kinder to busy people.

No-show penalty

Lose the credit only, or higher price / suspension? Beto: if paying again is enough, ghosting returns.

Monthly "new matches" notice to people already dating

Founder sees curiosity revenue; Beto warns of "mass cheating" and married users lying behind private profiles.

Income and occupation

Founder: income never allowed. Beto questioned it. Occupation — allowed or not?

Risks

Liquidity / cold start

Reciprocal matches on detailed descriptions need density. Launch one city; the "we found N" count will be 0 for many users at first — design the waitlist so waiting feels intentional.

Truthful counts and reviews

The call floated inventing review text and keeping counts vague. The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews, and invented "N people" counts are a deceptive pattern. Use real counts and real reviews only.

Store fees and rules

Unlocking a match is a digital good: on iOS/Android it generally must go through in-app purchase (15–30% fee). Dating apps also need reporting, blocking and moderation to pass review.

Safety without chat

Meeting a stranger with zero messaging needs extra safety: verified identities, public venues, share-your-date with a friend, easy reporting.

Known and Sitch move first

Both already ship voice/AI matchmaking without swiping. Speed to NYC and the meet-to-unlock mechanic are the moat.

Check-in disputes

One side can deny a meeting or file a false report. Two-sided QR check-in plus feedback history is the fix; still needs a human dispute path.
